How These Numbers Are Computed

Over all {{ "{:,}".format(n) }} {{ unit }} in scope, not just one page of rates. A row of the file is one price for one provider group and NPI, so a group listing many NPIs at one price repeats once per NPI. Count by decides what counts once: Provider counts each provider’s price once (EIN + price + group + file), NPI each clinician’s price once, Every row each row of the file. The medians differ because the weighting differs: by Provider every provider’s price counts once, whether it has one clinician or 500, while by NPI and Every row a price counts once per clinician, so a few large groups can pull the median toward their price. When both networks are searched, a provider’s same price in each counts once per network; pick one network under Search in to count it once.

No averages are shown: an average can be a price nobody negotiated. The median is the standard median (with an even number of values it is the midpoint of the two middle prices). Quartiles and percentiles interpolate between values the same way (Excel QUARTILE.INC). A price is an outlier when it lies more than 1.5 times the Q1–Q3 range below Q1 or above Q3: far from the middle half of prices. P99.9 needs at least 1,000 values and P99.99 10,000.

What explains price (Compare Groups) is the share of the variation in price, measured in percentages (a $10 vs $20 gap counts the same as $100 vs $200), that lies between groups when grouping by place of service, by modifier, and by both; the rest is within a group, mostly which provider. It is over every row of the code’s own rates.
